Slide 1: Introduction — Motivation, Novelty & Team Roles

Rubric: Introduction (1 Mark) Abhishekh Bhavya Vrushank Kapish
1. Layman Explanation (Plain English Analogy)

Imagine a surgeon performing robotic surgery or an engineer wearing a VR glove. A camera tracks the 21 joints of their hand. Suddenly, two things go wrong:

  • Surgical tools block the view (Occlusion): A scalpel or clamp hides 30% to 70% of the fingers from the camera.
  • Medical latex gloves shine under bright lights (Glare & Stiffness): Smooth latex hides skin creases, pulls fingers stiffly, and reflects surgical lights—making camera tracking dots jump wildly.

Standard AI trackers (like Google MediaPipe or Apple ARKit) panic when fingers are hidden: they guess 21 independent $X, Y, Z$ dots in mid-air. As a result, the virtual hand stretches like rubber (bones growing by $2\text{ cm}$) or bends fingers backward like a broken bone. Our Solution: Instead of guessing floating $X, Y, Z$ dots, we force the AI to operate an anatomical puppet with fixed metal bone lengths and mechanical knuckle stoppers that physically cannot stretch or bend backward.

2. Deep Conceptual & Mathematical Explanation
  • Unconstrained models regress coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^{21 \times 3}$ (63 independent numbers) with zero structural guarantee that parent-child bone distances remain invariant.
  • We replace direct coordinate regression with a 520K-parameter Spatio-Temporal Graph Autoencoder Teacher distilled into a 78.4K-parameter Edge Student MLP that predicts continuous 6D rotations on $\text{SO}(3)$, clamped via $\tanh$ Range-of-Motion limits and decoded via Differentiable Forward Kinematics, guaranteeing $0.0000\text{ mm}$ bone error and $100.0\%$ validity at $804.9\text{ FPS}$ on Apple Silicon MacBook Air.

Slide 2: Project Objectives & Literature Gap

Rubric: Objectives (1 Mark) Abhishekh Vrushank
1. Layman Explanation (Plain English Analogy)

Why did previous research papers fail to solve this?

  • Graph Models without Skeleton Locks (HandGCN, HOPE-Net): They connect knuckles in their middle layers, but their final output layer still guesses raw $X, Y, Z$ dots—so bones still stretch by $3.4\text{–}4.8\text{ mm}$.
  • Massive Transformers (HandOccNet, CVPR 2022): Great accuracy, but the model is huge ($37.2\text{ million}$ parameters, $142\text{ MB}$) and runs at $30\text{ FPS}$ on a desktop GPU. Our Student is $470\times$ smaller!
  • "Speed-Limit Sign" Soft Penalties (Spurr et al., ECCV 2020): During training, if the AI stretches a bone or bends a finger backward, they add a penalty score. The flaw: Soft penalties are just speed-limit signs—at test time, when half the hand is hidden, the AI still speeds past the sign ($8.6\%$ of hands are still broken, with $1.9\text{ mm}$ bone error). We install a physical governor on the engine (hard Forward Kinematics inside the forward pass) so breaking a bone is mathematically impossible.
2. Deep Conceptual & Mathematical Explanation
  • Prior works apply soft hinge regularizers $\mathcal{L}_{\text{bone}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{angle}}$ on unconstrained $\mathbb{R}^{21 \times 3}$ outputs. At test time under heavy occlusion, conflicting gradients cause anatomical violations.
  • Our architecture restricts the output space strictly to $\text{SO}(3)$ rotations passed through `BiomechanicalRoMClamper` and `DifferentiableFK`, achieving $0.0000\text{ mm}$ bone error and $100.0\%$ validity.

Slide 5: 6D Continuous Rotations & Hard Edge Forward Kinematics

Vrushank
1. Layman Explanation (Plain English Analogy)

How do we mathematically guarantee zero bone stretching and zero backward-bent knuckles?

  1. 6D Rotation Dials (Gram-Schmidt): Standard Euler angles hit "dead spots" (Gimbal lock) and Quaternions have $+180^\circ/-180^\circ$ jumps. Instead, our network outputs two 3D vectors (6 numbers) per joint. Gram-Schmidt makes them $90^\circ$ perpendicular and cross-multiplies them to get a smooth, jump-free $3\times 3$ rotation matrix!
  2. Soft Rubber Knuckle Bumpers (BiomechanicalRoMClamper): Your middle finger PIP knuckle bends inward up to $+110^\circ$, but cannot bend backward ($0^\circ$ limit). Instead of a sharp brick wall that kills training gradients, we pass angles through a smooth $\tanh$ S-curve that gently stops every joint inside human limits.
  3. Theorem 1 — Why Bone Error is Identically $0.0000\text{ mm}$: Starting at the wrist $(0,0,0)$, we rotate fixed canonical bone rods ($\mathbf{v}_j^{\text{rest}}$) from parent to child. Because rotating a rigid metal rod never changes its length ($\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{v}\| = \|\mathbf{v}\| = L_j$), bone-length error is identically $0.0000\text{ mm}$!
2. Deep Conceptual & Mathematical Explanation

$$\mathbf{b}_1 = \frac{\mathbf{a}_1}{\|\mathbf{a}_1\|_2}, \quad \mathbf{b}_2 = \frac{\mathbf{a}_2 - (\mathbf{b}_1 \cdot \mathbf{a}_2)\mathbf{b}_1}{\|\mathbf{a}_2 - (\mathbf{b}_1 \cdot \mathbf{a}_2)\mathbf{b}_1\|_2}, \quad \mathbf{b}_3 = \mathbf{b}_1 \times \mathbf{b}_2, \quad \boldsymbol{\theta}_j^{\text{clamp}} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_j + \boldsymbol{\Delta}_j \odot \tanh\!\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\theta}_j - \boldsymbol{\mu}_j}{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_j \cdot \tau + \epsilon}\right)$$

Theorem 1 Proof: $\|\mathbf{P}_j - \mathbf{P}_{p(j)}\|_2 = \|\mathbf{R}_j^{\text{global}} \mathbf{v}_j^{\text{rest}}\|_2 = \sqrt{(\mathbf{v}_j^{\text{rest}})^\top (\mathbf{R}_j^{\text{global}})^\top \mathbf{R}_j^{\text{global}} \mathbf{v}_j^{\text{rest}}} = \|\mathbf{v}_j^{\text{rest}}\|_2 = L_j \implies \varepsilon_{\text{bone}} \equiv 0.0000\text{ mm}$.

3. Exact Speaker Script & Pointers

Vrushank (6:00–6:45): "Slide 5 presents our hard kinematic manifold in models/kinematics.py. First, the network predicts 6 unconstrained scalars per joint, which we orthogonalize into a valid 3x3 rotation matrix in SO(3) via Gram-Schmidt normalization. Second, BiomechanicalRoMClamper extracts local flexion, twist, and abduction angles using a C1-smooth small-angle polynomial transition near zero—avoiding the infinite derivative of arccos at theta=0—and bounds every angle inside its anatomical interval via tanh. PIP and DIP hinge joints are strictly clamped to [0, 110] and [0, 80] degrees of flexion with zero dorsal hyperextension."

Vrushank (6:45–7:30): "Third, DifferentiableFK accumulates global rotation matrices along PARENT_INDICES from the wrist out to the fingertips and rotates the fixed canonical bone vectors. Because any matrix R in SO(3) is an isometry, the distance between parent and child joints is identically L_j. That gives us Theorem 1: 0.0000 mm bone-length error and 100.0% biomechanical validity across all test frames, verified in our bare-metal NumPy EdgeFK engine running at 13.79 kHz on Apple Silicon."

Slide 6: Spatio-Temporal Graph Autoencoder Teacher

Abhishekh
1. Layman Explanation (Plain English Analogy)

How does our 520K-parameter Teacher network reconstruct fingers that are completely hidden behind a surgical tool?

  1. Smart Rest-Hand Fill-In (Imputation): Instead of feeding raw zeros $(0,0,0)$ when a joint is hidden ($m=0$), we plug in the joint's position from a neutral resting hand plus its visibility flag.
  2. "Mute the Hidden Knuckles" (Visibility Key Penalty $-\gamma_h(1-m_{t,j})$): In a Graph Network, joints pass messages to neighbors along the 20 bones and 4 palm connections. If a joint is hidden ($m=0$), we turn down its outgoing microphone (subtracting $\gamma_h$ when it acts as a Key) so it doesn't broadcast fake placeholder noise to visible knuckles, while keeping its incoming headphones open (as a Query) so it can listen to visible knuckles!
  3. Time Memory (Bi-GRU across 8 Frames): Looking at an 8-frame video clip lets the network see where the fingers were moving right before the tool blocked them—cutting 50%-occlusion error by $22.6\%$ ($8.41 \to 6.51\text{ mm}$) and cutting jitter by $41.1\%$ compared to a single-frame spatial network!
2. Deep Conceptual & Mathematical Explanation

$$e_{t,i,j}^{(h)} = \frac{\mathbf{q}_{t,i}^{(h)} \cdot \mathbf{k}_{t,j}^{(h)}}{\sqrt{d_h}} + 0.25\,\text{LeakyReLU}\!\left(\mathbf{a}_{\text{src}}^{(h)}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{t,i}^{(h)} + \mathbf{a}_{\text{dst}}^{(h)}\cdot\mathbf{k}_{t,j}^{(h)}\right) + B_{h,i,j} - \gamma_h(1 - m_{t,j}), \quad \alpha_{t,i,j}^{(h)} = \text{softmax}_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)}\!\left(e_{t,i,j}^{(h)}\right)$$

3. Exact Speaker Script & Pointers

Abhishekh (7:30–8:15): "Slide 6 details the Spatio-Temporal Graph Autoencoder Teacher in models/teacher_st_gae.py. When a surgical instrument masks a finger subtree, its input coordinates are zeroed (m=0). Before entering the graph layers, we impute masked coordinates with canonical rest positions and construct a 7-dimensional feature vector combining imputed position, binary visibility, and visible displacement from rest, summed with a learned joint-identity embedding and a global posture skip. We then pass the 21 nodes through three 4-head Graph Attention layers masked by the anatomical adjacency matrix—comprising the 20 bone segments plus 4 inter-MCP palmar arch connections."

Abhishekh (8:15–9:00): "A key design choice in MultiHeadGraphAttentionLayer is visibility-aware key gating: we subtract a learnable penalty gamma_h * (1 - m) from the pre-softmax attention logits whenever key node j is occluded. This forces occluded distal phalanges to attend strongly to visible proximal knuckles and neighboring fingers across the palmar arch rather than propagating corrupted zeros. Next, our temporal core applies a depthwise 1D convolution and a Bidirectional GRU across the T=8 window, reducing 50%-occlusion MPJPE from 8.41 mm in our SpatialOnlyGAE ablation down to 6.51 mm in the full Teacher—a 22.6% overall error reduction while cutting temporal acceleration jitter by 41.1%."

Slide 7: Physics-Informed Knowledge Distillation & Edge Student Compression

Kapish
1. Layman Explanation (Plain English Analogy)
  • Why Compress? The Teacher takes $22.4\text{ million}$ math operations (FLOPs) per frame. To run in ~1 millisecond on lightweight laptops and edge chips, we build StudentKinematicMLP (just 78,398 parameters—$6.64\times$ smaller and $137.3\times$ fewer FLOPs!).
  • How the Student Works: Replaces heavy Graph Attention and sequential GRU loops with a flat linear layer + a single fast 1D Temporal Convolution (TemporalMixingBlock1D) feeding into the exact same 6D Forward Kinematics layer.
  • 5-Part Teacher Coaching (DistillationLoss): The Student is trained simultaneously to match (1) the Teacher's 6D rotations + 3D geodesic angles, (2) the Teacher's 3D joint positions, (3) true Ground-Truth 3D positions ($1.5\times$ boost on hidden joints), (4) true Ground-Truth rotations, and (5) smooth velocity/acceleration over time.
2. Deep Conceptual & Mathematical Explanation

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = 35.0\,\mathcal{L}_{6\text{D}}(\hat{\mathbf{R}}_{\text{S}}, \mathbf{R}_{\text{T}}) + 0.45\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{MPJPE}}^w(\hat{\mathbf{P}}_{\text{S}}, \mathbf{P}_{\text{T}}) + 0.55\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{MPJPE}}^w(\hat{\mathbf{P}}_{\text{S}}, \mathbf{P}_{\text{GT}}) + 25.0\,\mathcal{L}_{6\text{D}}(\hat{\mathbf{R}}_{\text{S}}, \mathbf{R}_{\text{GT}}) + 0.35\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}$$

3. Exact Speaker Script & Pointers

Kapish (9:00–9:40): "Slide 7 shows how we compress Abhishekh's 520K-parameter Teacher into our 78,398-parameter StudentKinematicMLP.
